01THE LEGAL FRAMEWORK

What the law requires
and how it is organised.

The Strata Titles Act sets out the corporation’s structure, meetings, decision-making and recordkeeping. These rules apply whether day-to-day administration is handled by owners, a committee or a manager.

01 / AUTOMATIC STRUCTUREOne scheme.
One corporation.

A strata corporation is formed when the strata plan is lodged. It is responsible for managing the common property and applying the scheme’s rules.

SA.GOV.AU / STRATA TITLES ↗
02 / EVERY YEAROne meeting
each calendar year.

The corporation must hold at least one annual general meeting in every calendar year, and no more than 15 months after the last one.

STRATA TITLES ACT 1988 / S 33(4) ↗
03 / KEEP THE RECORDDecisions
need a record.

Section 40 requires a minute book for corporation meetings and proper accounting records. A management committee, if appointed, must also minute its proceedings.

STRATA TITLES ACT 1988 / SS 35, 40 ↗
i

A useful distinction: a management committee is optional under the Strata Titles Act. A corporation can run its business through general meetings. If it appoints a committee, that committee must keep minutes of its proceedings too.

02HOW THE FRAMEWORK WORKS

A few duties sit behind
every formal decision.

These are the practical mechanics that shape how a South Australian strata corporation operates.

A / OFFICERS

Three roles must be filled

A strata corporation must have a presiding officer, secretary and treasurer. The same person may hold more than one office, and the offices cannot remain vacant for more than six months.

S 23 ↗
B / AGM AGENDA

Annual meeting, defined business

The agenda includes a motion to confirm the previous general meeting’s minutes and the text of proposed special or unanimous resolutions. The AGM agenda also covers the previous period’s accounts, current-period contributions and required insurance policies.

S 33(4) ↗
C / GENERAL MEETINGS

Notice, agenda, quorum

Owners must receive written notice at least 14 days ahead. The notice includes an agenda. For business to proceed, voting power for at least half the units must be represented; if a quorum is absent, the Act sets out how the meeting is adjourned and reconvened.

S 33(3–6) ↗
D / COMMITTEE MEETINGS

If a committee is appointed

The corporation may appoint a management committee by ordinary resolution. Committee meetings require at least three days’ notice, decisions are made by majority, and minutes must be kept.

S 35 ↗
E / CORPORATION RECORDS

More than meeting minutes

The recordkeeping duty also covers accounting records, statements of account, notices and orders, correspondence, meeting notices and other prescribed documents. Unit holders and prospective purchasers can request specified records under the Act.

SS 39A–41 ↗
F / RETENTION

A long memory is required

Meeting minute books must be retained for 30 years. Accounting records, statements of account, correspondence, notices and meeting notices generally have a seven-year retention period.

REG 30 ↗
G / ACCESS TO RECORDS

Records can be requested

Owners, mortgagees and prospective purchasers can apply for specified information and records. The corporation generally has five business days to respond; meeting-minute copies may cover a requested period of up to two years, and prescribed fees can apply.

S 41 ↗
H / PROPERTY & CONTRIBUTIONS

Maintenance connects to money

The corporation’s functions include administering and maintaining common property. It may raise contributions by ordinary resolution; the usual basis is unit entitlement. Using another basis requires a unanimous resolution.

SS 25, 27 ↗

IN SUMMARY

A strata scheme is a shared asset
and a shared administrative system.

The law defines the corporation, the meetings, the roles and the records. How communities make that work in practice is the question at the heart of the problem.
