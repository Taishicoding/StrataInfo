## Legal structure and roles

### LS-01 | When is a strata corporation created in South Australia, and what does it own or manage?

- Keywords: creation; incorporation; strata plan; common property; ownership
- Legislation references supplied: Strata Titles Act 1988 (SA), sections 10 and 18.
- Additional source supplied: Legal Services Commission of South Australia, Strata Titles Booklet, pages 5–6.
- Sources: SRC-01; SRC-03; SRC-08 (incorrectly targeted supplied link).
- Version supplied: Current consolidated version, authorised 9 December 2021.

#### Answer as supplied

A strata corporation is established automatically upon the deposit of a strata plan in the Lands Titles Registration Office. It is named “Strata Corporation No. [number] Incorporated”.

The corporation holds the common property in trust for all unit owners as tenants in common. Examples include driveways, exterior grounds, stairwells, and shared structural elements. It manages, maintains, and repairs common property and enforces the corporation’s articles (rules).

Since 1 June 2009, new subdivisions in South Australia are created under the Community Titles Act 1996, while existing strata schemes continue under the 1988 Act.

### LS-02 | Which officer roles must every strata corporation have?

- Keywords: officers; presiding officer; secretary; treasurer; eligibility
- Legislation references supplied: Strata Titles Act 1988 (SA), section 23(1).
- Sources: SRC-01.
- Version supplied: Current consolidated version, authorised 9 December 2021.

#### Answer as supplied

Every strata corporation must appoint three mandatory officers:

1. Presiding officer: chairs and conducts general meetings.
2. Secretary: manages administrative correspondence, notices, minutes, and records.
3. Treasurer: manages bank accounts, financial records, budgets, and levy collection.

Officers must be unit holders or, where a body corporate owns a unit, a nominated director or representative.

### LS-03 | Can one person hold more than one officer role?

- Keywords: multiple roles; combined offices; small schemes
- Legislation references supplied: Strata Titles Act 1988 (SA), section 23(2).
- Sources: SRC-01.
- Version supplied: Current consolidated version, authorised 9 December 2021.

#### Answer as supplied

Yes. A single unit holder may hold two or all three officer positions concurrently, including presiding officer, secretary, and treasurer. The supplied summary describes this as common practice in small schemes of two to six units.

### LS-04 | Is a management committee mandatory, or can the corporation make decisions through general meetings?

- Keywords: optional committee; general meetings; ordinary resolution; self-management
- Legislation references supplied: Strata Titles Act 1988 (SA), section 35(1).
- Additional source supplied: Legal Services Commission, Law Handbook, Management Committee.
- Sources: SRC-01; SRC-04.
- Version supplied: Current consolidated version, authorised 9 December 2021.

#### Answer as supplied

A management committee is not mandatory. Section 35(1) states that a strata corporation may appoint one by ordinary resolution.

Without a committee, the corporation governs itself directly through unit owners voting at general meetings or through unanimous written resolutions.

### LS-05 | If a management committee is appointed, how is it formed and what decisions can it make?

- Keywords: committee election; membership; delegated powers; reserved decisions
- Legislation references supplied: Strata Titles Act 1988 (SA), sections 35(1)–(3); Schedule 3 (Model Articles).
- Sources: SRC-01.
- Version supplied: Current consolidated version, authorised 9 December 2021.

#### Answer as supplied

Formation:

- Elected by ordinary resolution at an annual general meeting (AGM) or another general meeting.
- Consists of the appointed officers—presiding officer, secretary, and treasurer—and any other elected unit owners.

Decision powers:

- Can make routine administrative, operational, and day-to-day maintenance decisions on behalf of the corporation.
- Cannot decide matters reserved for the full corporation by the Act, articles, or a general meeting resolution.
- Examples of reserved matters supplied include altering articles, structural common-property changes, changing unit entitlements, and decisions requiring special or unanimous resolutions.

### LS-06 | What responsibilities remain with the strata corporation if it appoints a strata manager?

- Keywords: strata manager; delegation; liability; management contract; retained responsibilities
- Legislation references supplied: Strata Titles Act 1988 (SA), sections 27A and 27B(3)–(5).
- Additional source supplied: Legal Services Commission, Law Handbook, Strata Managers.
- Sources: SRC-01; SRC-05.
- Version supplied: Current consolidated version, authorised 9 December 2021.

#### Answer as supplied

Appointing a professional strata manager (body corporate manager) delegates administrative tasks rather than transferring legal liability. The corporation retains ultimate legal responsibility for compliance with the Act.

- Decisions requiring special or unanimous resolutions cannot be delegated to a manager.
- Required officer positions must still be maintained.
- The corporation remains legally accountable for statutory duties, financial solvency, building maintenance, and adequate insurance.
- A management contract must be written, approved by ordinary resolution, and comply with statutory term and lock-in limits.

Cross-reference: MD-06 separately reproduces a supplied claim that manager contracts require a special resolution. These supplied statements conflict; neither has been verified here.

## Meetings and decisions

### MD-01 | How often must a strata corporation hold an annual general meeting?

- Keywords: AGM; annual meeting; calendar year; 15 months
- Legislation references supplied: Strata Titles Act 1988 (SA), section 33(1).
- Sources: SRC-01.
- Version supplied: Current consolidated version, authorised 9 December 2021.

#### Answer as supplied

An AGM must be held at least once in each calendar year and no more than 15 months after the previous AGM.

### MD-02 | How much notice must owners receive for a general meeting, and what must the notice include?

- Keywords: meeting notice; 14 days; agenda; financial statements; budget
- Legislation references supplied: Strata Titles Act 1988 (SA), sections 33(3)–(4a); Strata Titles Regulations 2018 (SA), regulation 17.
- Sources: SRC-01; SRC-02.
- Version supplied: Current consolidated version, authorised 9 December 2021.

#### Answer as supplied

At least 14 days’ written notice must be given to every unit holder before any general meeting.

The notice must include:

- Date, time, and venue, or physical/electronic format.
- Agenda of business to be conducted.
- Exact text of any proposed special or unanimous resolution.
- For an AGM, financial statements for the preceding financial year and a proposed budget or statement of expenditure for the coming year.

### MD-03 | What quorum is required for a general meeting, and what happens if it is not reached?

- Keywords: quorum; half of units; proxy; adjournment; 30 minutes
- Legislation references supplied: Strata Titles Act 1988 (SA), sections 33(5)–(6).
- Sources: SRC-01.
- Version supplied: Current consolidated version, authorised 9 December 2021.

#### Answer as supplied

Quorum requires persons entitled to exercise voting rights for at least one-half (50%) of all units to be present in person or represented by proxy.

If quorum is absent within 30 minutes of the scheduled start:

1. The meeting stands adjourned to the same day the following week at the same time and place, or to a date at least seven but no more than 14 days away.
2. If quorum is still absent within 30 minutes at the adjourned meeting, the unit holders present and entitled to vote constitute a valid quorum.

### MD-04 | What rules apply to committee meeting notice, quorum, voting and minutes?

- Keywords: committee notice; committee quorum; majority; casting vote; minute book
- Legislation references supplied: Strata Titles Act 1988 (SA), sections 35(4)–(7) and 40(1).
- Sources: SRC-01.
- Version supplied: Current consolidated version, authorised 9 December 2021.

#### Answer as supplied

- Notice: Reasonable written notice specifying the time and place must be given to all committee members.
- Quorum: At least half the total committee members must be present.
- Voting: Simple majority of votes cast by members present. Each member has one vote. The presiding officer has a deliberative vote only, with no casting vote.
- Minutes: Comprehensive written minutes recording all resolutions and decisions must be kept in the corporation’s minute book.

### MD-05 | How can a strata corporation make decisions between meetings, if at all?

- Keywords: between meetings; written resolution; EGM; urgent decisions; requisition
- Legislation references supplied: Strata Titles Act 1988 (SA), sections 33(2) and 35(3).
- Additional source supplied: Legal Services Commission, Strata Titles Booklet, page 10.
- Sources: SRC-01; SRC-03.
- Version supplied: Current consolidated version, authorised 9 December 2021.

#### Answer as supplied

1. Management committee: An elected committee can make operational and day-to-day decisions within its delegated scope between general meetings.
2. Unanimous written resolution: A corporation decision can be made without a meeting if a written resolution is circulated and consented to or signed by all unit owners entitled to vote.
3. Extraordinary general meeting (EGM): An urgent matter requiring a vote can be dealt with at an EGM requisitioned by the secretary, committee, or unit holders representing at least one-third of all units, under section 33(2).

### MD-06 | When are ordinary, special or unanimous resolutions required?

- Keywords: resolution thresholds; ordinary; special; unanimous; voting; articles
- Legislation references supplied: Strata Titles Act 1988 (SA), sections 3(1), 12, 19(2), 27, 30, and 34.
- Additional source supplied: Legal Services Commission, Law Handbook, Types of Resolutions.
- Sources: SRC-01; SRC-06.
- Version supplied: Current consolidated version, authorised 9 December 2021.

#### Answer as supplied: Ordinary resolution

Threshold: A simple majority, over 50% of votes cast at a general meeting.

Examples supplied:

- Routine administrative decisions.
- Adopting annual accounts.
- Electing officers or committee members.
- Approving the annual budget.
- Standard repairs within budget.

#### Answer as supplied: Special resolution

Threshold: At least 14 days’ written notice stating the proposed resolution; a majority of at least two-thirds (66.7%) of votes cast; votes against must not exceed 25% of the total number of units.

Examples supplied:

- Altering articles or rules, section 19(2).
- Entering or renewing a strata manager contract, section 27B.
- Granting exclusive use of common property.
- Substantial additions or alterations to common property.

Cross-reference: LS-06 instead states that a management contract requires an ordinary resolution. This inconsistency is retained for verification.

#### Answer as supplied: Unanimous resolution

Threshold: 100% agreement of votes cast, with no dissenting votes.

Examples supplied:

- Changing unit entitlements or amending the strata plan, section 12.
- Levying contributions on a basis other than unit entitlement, section 27(3).
- Dispensing with statutory building insurance, section 30(2).
- Altering unit boundaries.

## Minutes and records

### MR-01 | What records must a strata corporation keep?

- Keywords: records; accounts; register; insurance; correspondence; minutes
- Legislation references supplied: Strata Titles Act 1988 (SA), section 40(1); Strata Titles Regulations 2018 (SA), regulation 23.
- Sources: SRC-01; SRC-02.
- Version supplied: Current consolidated version, authorised 9 December 2021.

#### Answer as supplied

- Minute books recording all general and committee meetings.
- Proper accounting records showing receipts, expenditures, assets, and liabilities.
- Books of account and annual financial statements.
- Register of unit holders, including contact details and mortgagee notices.
- Current insurance policies, renewal certificates, and premium receipts.
- Contracts, notices, and copies of official correspondence.

### MR-02 | What information should meeting minutes contain, and how are they confirmed?

- Keywords: minute contents; motions; voting outcomes; confirmation; signature
- Legislation references supplied: Strata Titles Act 1988 (SA), section 40(1).
- Additional source supplied: Legal Services Commission, Strata Titles Booklet, pages 11–12.
- Sources: SRC-01; SRC-03.
- Version supplied: Current consolidated version, authorised 9 December 2021.

#### Answer as supplied

Minute contents:

- Meeting date, time, and location.
- Names of unit holders attending in person, apologies, and appointed proxies.
- Exact text of every proposed motion or resolution.
- Voting outcome: carried, lost, or withdrawn; whether ordinary, special, or unanimous.
- Financial approvals and levy amounts.

Confirmation:

Draft minutes are circulated to owners after the meeting. They are formally submitted for confirmation at the next meeting, confirmed by resolution, and signed by the presiding officer.

### MR-03 | How long must minutes and other records be retained?

- Keywords: retention; seven years; permanent records; invoices; strata plan
- Legislation references supplied: Strata Titles Act 1988 (SA), section 40(2); Strata Titles Regulations 2018 (SA), regulation 23.
- Sources: SRC-01; SRC-02.
- Version supplied: Current consolidated version, authorised 9 December 2021.

#### Answer as supplied

- Financial and accounting records: Receipts, invoices, bank statements, and accounting books must be kept for at least seven years.
- Minutes and foundational records: Meeting minutes, strata plan, registered articles, and register of unit holders must be retained permanently as continuous corporation records.

### MR-04 | Who can inspect or request copies of minutes, accounts and other strata records?

- Keywords: inspection; copies; purchaser; mortgagee; authorised agent; five business days
- Legislation references supplied: Strata Titles Act 1988 (SA), section 41(1); Strata Titles Regulations 2018 (SA), regulation 31.
- Sources: SRC-01; SRC-02.
- Version supplied: Current consolidated version, authorised 9 December 2021.

#### Answer as supplied

Access may be requested by:

- Any unit holder.
- A prospective purchaser or contract purchaser of a unit.
- A registered mortgagee of a unit.
- An authorised agent, such as a conveyancer, solicitor, or commercial search agent, acting for an owner, purchaser, or mortgagee.

Following written application and payment of the prescribed fee, the corporation must provide the records or information within five business days.

## Day-to-day responsibilities

### DR-01 | Who is responsible for maintaining and repairing common property?

- Keywords: maintenance; repairs; common property; private interiors; shared services
- Legislation references supplied: Strata Titles Act 1988 (SA), sections 25 and 28.
- Additional source supplied: Legal Services Commission, Law Handbook, Maintenance.
- Sources: SRC-01; SRC-04 (also supplied for maintenance).
- Version supplied: Current consolidated version, authorised 9 December 2021.

#### Answer as supplied

The corporation must maintain, repair, and keep all common property clean and functional.

Examples supplied include shared driveways, external grounds, boundary fences, roofs, gutters, and utility service lines serving multiple units.

Individual unit holders maintain internal fixtures, fittings, and non-structural interior components of their units.

### DR-02 | What insurance must a strata corporation maintain?

- Keywords: insurance; reinstatement; public liability; fidelity guarantee; workers compensation
- Legislation references supplied: Strata Titles Act 1988 (SA), sections 30, 31(1), and 31(2a); Strata Titles Regulations 2018 (SA), regulation 14.
- Sources: SRC-01; SRC-02.
- Version supplied: Current consolidated version, authorised 9 December 2021.

#### Answer as supplied

1. Building and improvement insurance, section 30: Full replacement and reinstatement value insurance for all buildings and improvements on the strata parcel, covering fire, storm, and other usual risks.
2. Public liability insurance, section 31(1): Mandatory public risk cover for death, personal injury, and property damage claims arising from common property. The supplied summary states a statutory minimum of AUD 10,000,000 and describes AUD 20,000,000 as standard modern practice.
3. Fidelity guarantee insurance, section 31(2a) and regulation 14: Required if the corporation maintains an administrative or sinking fund, to cover fraud, theft, or dishonest misappropriation of corporation funds.
4. Workers’ compensation: Compulsory where the corporation directly employs maintenance, gardening, or cleaning personnel.

### DR-03 | How are contributions, levies and shared expenses decided and recorded?

- Keywords: contributions; levies; budget; administrative fund; sinking fund; unit entitlement
- Legislation references supplied: Strata Titles Act 1988 (SA), sections 27(1)–(3) and 33(4); section 40 for accounting records.
- Sources: SRC-01.
- Version supplied: Current consolidated version, authorised 9 December 2021.

#### Answer as supplied

Decision:

Contributions are determined by ordinary resolution at an AGM or general meeting, using an itemised annual budget establishing:

- Administrative fund: Day-to-day recurrent operational costs.
- Sinking fund: Long-term capital repair and replacement.

Apportionment:

Under section 27(3), levies are allocated strictly according to each unit’s unit entitlement on the registered strata plan, unless all owners agree otherwise by unanimous resolution.

Recording:

Levies are entered in accounting records under section 40. Formal written contribution notices state each owner’s amount, due dates, and payment methods.

### DR-04 | What are the usual steps for reporting, approving, arranging and documenting a repair?

- Keywords: repair workflow; reporting; quotes; approval; contractor; payment; warranties
- Basis supplied: Practical governance workflow aligned with Strata Titles Act 1988 (SA), sections 25, 27B, 35, and 40; Consumer and Business Services SA and Legal Services Commission practical guidance.
- Sources: SRC-01; SRC-03; SRC-07.
- Version supplied: Practical operational workflow aligned with legislation authorised 9 December 2021.
- Content type: Practical workflow as supplied; not presented as verbatim statutory requirements.

#### Answer as supplied

1. Reporting: An owner or tenant reports a common-property issue or hazard in writing to the secretary or manager, including a description and photographs.
2. Assessment and quoting: The corporation or manager determines whether the item is common property or private unit property. Quotes are obtained from licensed and insured contractors. The supplied summary describes two or three quotes for significant expenditure as standard industry practice.
3. Approval:
   - Within pre-authorised emergency delegation or committee spending limits: Committee or manager approves directly.
   - Above delegated thresholds or outside budget: Approval is obtained by ordinary resolution at a general meeting or EGM.
4. Arranging works: The approved tradesperson is formally engaged, works are scheduled, and affected residents receive reasonable advance notice of necessary property access.
5. Documentation and payment: After completion and inspection, the invoice is verified and paid from the administrative or sinking fund. Work orders, invoices, and warranties are filed under section 40.
